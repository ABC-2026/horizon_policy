Why a Hybrid Business Model?

The report says you should make money in three ways, not just by selling a robot.

1. One-Time Deployment Fee (Large Upfront Payment)

When a lab buys your solution, they pay for:

Gantry hardware
Cameras
Installation
Integration with their LIS (Laboratory Information System)
Initial setup and training

Example:

Hardware + deployment = ₹15–30 lakh

This is your initial revenue.

2. Recurring Software Subscription (Monthly/Yearly)

The robot is only useful because of the software and AI.

Labs pay continuously for:

VLA updates
Workflow improvements
Analytics
Monitoring
Remote support

Example:

₹50,000–₹2 lakh per month

This becomes predictable recurring revenue.

3. Service & Support Revenue

Labs need reliability because every minute of downtime affects patient reports.

Offer support plans such as:
Tier	Example
Basic	Business-hours support
Premium	24/7 support
Enterprise	Dedicated engineer + fast response
Labs pay annually for these plans.

A gantry does this very well.
So instead of building a humanoid robot on day one, you start with a gantry system and add intelligence through the VLA.

2. RaaS Model (Robot as a Service)
Instead of selling the robot:
You install it for free or low cost.
Customer pays monthly.
Example:
₹1 lakh/month
Why labs like it
No big upfront payment.
Instead of:
₹25 lakh today
They pay:
₹1 lakh/month
Much easier to approve.
and it will also enrich the robotics ecosystem as new player will be able to emerge as there is no huge capex or barrier to entry in labs so more labs enriches the ecosystem 



disadvantages of raas model 
Why startups don't like it
You still own the robot.
So if:
Robot breaks
Customer cancels
Robot sits unused
You lose money.
The startup takes all the risk.
Example
You build robot:
Cost = ₹20 lakh
Customer pays:
₹1 lakh/month
You need 20+ months just to recover hardware cost.
If customer leaves after 6 months:
You lose money.
this is why we stay genral with our tasks cause even if a partner leaves we still can just use the robot for something else just by finetuning it for a different process for a different vendor thats the magic of a vla

What is a Pre-Agreed Conversion Path?
Before the pilot starts, both sides agree:
"If these results are achieved, here's what happens next."
Example:
Before pilot:
"If robot reduces handling time by 40% and error rate by 30%, the lab will purchase 3 systems."
Everything is agreed upfront.
No surprises later.

What does "Robot Novelty" mean?
Novelty means:
Something interesting because it is new.
Example:
"Wow! A robot moving test tubes!"
That's novelty.
But labs do not buy novelty.
They buy ROI.
Return on Investment.

Example:
Customer doesn't say:
"This robot is cool."
Customer says:
"Will this reduce errors by 30%?"
What are Failure Points?
Failure point means:
Any place where something can go wrong.

Examples:
Technician forgets scan
Sample goes to wrong analyzer
Tube misplaced
Label damaged
Rack misidentified

Automation reduces failure points.
